Ask anyone who has looked for work in India recently and you hear the same story. They applied to sixty roles across four **job search websites** and got three replies. Not because their CV was weak. Because most of those openings were already gone.

This is the honest problem with **job search sites** in 2026, and it is worth understanding before you spend another month of evenings on it.

## Why most job search websites waste your time

Nearly every **job search engine** competes on volume. Bigger number, better site, so the thinking goes. But a listing is only worth your evening if the role is still open, the apply link still works, and someone will actually read what you send.

Most **job listing websites** have no mechanism for the first two. A posting goes up, it stays up, and nobody checks it again. Some sites go further and re-date old postings to the top of the feed so the page looks busy. You apply to something advertised as posted today that was actually published in August.

We think that is the single biggest reason job hunting in India feels like shouting into a well.

## How to judge job search websites in two minutes

Whichever **job finding websites** you end up using, these four checks will tell you most of what you need to know.

**Open a listing and look at the posted date.** Then open the company's own careers page and find the same role. If the dates differ by weeks, the site is re-dating old postings.

**Click apply.** If it takes you to another page on the same job site rather than to the employer, you are in a funnel, not a job search.

**Look for a closed-jobs page.** Almost no site has one. A site willing to show you what it removed is a site that removes things.

**Check whether money is ever mentioned.** A registration fee, a security deposit, a training charge, a refundable amount. Any of these means stop. We wrote more about this in our guide to [spotting a fake job posting](/insights/blogs/trusted-job-sites-india).
