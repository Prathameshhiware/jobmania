You cleared the interviews. You got the letter. Everyone congratulated you. And then nothing happened for four months.

If that is where you are, the first thing worth knowing is that it is not about you.

## How common this has become

Business Today reported in March 2026 that **around 1,000 candidates were waiting on joining letters at Tech Mahindra**, and that **more than 200 recruits at Wipro had their onboarding deferred**. Those are single company figures, reported in passing, in an article about something else.

The context is that fresher intake across Indian IT has fallen hard. Staffing analytics firm Xpheno puts fresher hiring at about 6 lakh in FY22 and about 1.2 lakh in FY25. Wipro cut its own fresher guidance from about 10,000 to between 7,500 and 8,000.

When a company announces a hiring number at the start of a year and the business does not arrive to justify it, the offers have usually already gone out. Deferred onboarding is what that gap looks like from the candidate's side.

## What the delay is actually telling you

An offer letter is a commitment to hire. A joining date is a commitment to deploy you on something.

Companies hire freshers in batches against expected project work. If that work is delayed or does not materialise, the company is left with people it has promised to employ and nowhere to put them. Keeping you on the bench costs money, so onboarding slips instead.

That means a delay is usually a signal about the company's order book, not about your performance in the interview. It also means the delay can continue for as long as that order book stays soft, which is why an open ended wait is the thing to be cautious about rather than the delay itself.

## What to ask, and how

Put all of this in email rather than on a phone call. You want a record, and written answers are more careful than spoken ones.

**Ask for an expected joining window.** Not a promise, a window. "Are you able to share an expected joining window, even an approximate one?" is a reasonable and answerable question.

**Ask whether the offer stays valid if that window passes.** This is the question most candidates do not ask, and it is the one that matters most.

**Ask whether there is any stipend or retainer during the wait.** Some companies pay a small retainer to deferred candidates. Most do not, but it costs nothing to ask.

**Ask whether there is any pre joining training you can complete.** Candidates who finish the company's own onboarding modules sometimes get called in earlier, because they need less ramp up time.

If HR will not answer any of these in writing after a reasonable follow up, treat that as information too.

## What to do while you wait

**Do not stop applying.** This is the single most expensive mistake in this situation. The letter feels like an ending, so people stop looking, and then four months later they are starting from zero with a gap on their CV. An offer letter with no joining date is not employment. Keep your search running until you have a confirmed date in writing.

**Apply off campus in parallel.** If this offer came through campus placements, the campus process is finished for you as far as the cell is concerned. [Off campus drives](/c/off-campus) run on their own schedule.

**Look at employers that are growing rather than contracting.** Global Capability Centres raised their share of entry level hiring in India from 8 per cent to 11 per cent in a year while IT services fell from 32 per cent to 24 per cent. They are hiring into their own order books, not against speculative project work. Our [GCC guide for freshers](/insights/playbook/how-to-get-into-a-gcc-as-a-fresher) covers how to apply.

**Take a temporary role if you need income.** India is adding up to 2.5 lakh seasonal roles before December, covered in [festive season jobs 2026](/insights/blogs/festive-season-jobs-2026-india). Check your offer letter for an exclusivity clause first, which most fresher offers do not have, and be straightforward with the temporary employer about your situation.

**Consider a walk-in.** A [walk-in interview](/c/walk-ins) often gives you an answer the same day, which is the opposite of what you are currently experiencing.

## How to read the risk honestly

The Unstop Talent Report 2026, which surveyed more than 37,000 students, found undergraduate candidates faced a **17 per cent rate of offer disruption**. That covers withdrawn offers as well as delayed ones.

So roughly one in six is a reasonable way to think about it. Not likely, but not rare enough to plan around as though it will not happen to you. Plan for the offer to arrive and keep a parallel path running in case it does not. Those are not contradictory.

## One thing that is never normal

A company asking you for money at any point in this process. Not a registration fee, not a security deposit, not a training charge, not a refundable amount to hold your offer.

No legitimate employer in India charges a candidate to be hired. If a request for money appears while you are waiting on a joining date, that is not your employer asking, whatever the email address looks like. Verify by calling the number on the company's own website rather than any number sent to you.
