Two numbers from the same survey, and holding both in your head at once tells you most of what you need to know about this placement season.

**85 per cent of engineering students graduating in 2026 are unplaced.** **87.8 per cent of companies say they are hiring.**

Both come from the Unstop Talent Report 2026, which surveyed more than 37,000 students and over 500 HR leaders between January and February. The report also found 84 per cent of undergraduate students and 74 per cent of MBA students still without an offer.

Companies are hiring. Students are not getting placed. Those are not contradictions, and the reason they coexist is worth understanding before you decide what to do next.

## The gap is about access, not ability

The single most useful finding in the report is this one.

Students at institutions that host **more than 150 recruiters a year** are about **2.9 times more likely** to be placed than students at campuses with limited recruiter presence.

Read that again, because it reframes the problem. A company that is hiring has to physically arrive at your college, or run a process you can find from outside it. If it does neither, its hiring intent is irrelevant to you no matter how genuine it is.

Most placement advice is about improving yourself. This number says a large part of the outcome was decided by which campus you are sitting on, before you did anything.

That is not a reason to give up. It is a reason to stop waiting for recruiters to come to you.

## Why engineering is hit hardest

The 85 per cent figure for engineers is not an accident of this one survey. It lines up with what has happened to IT hiring.

Staffing analytics firm Xpheno puts fresher hiring across Indian IT at about 6 lakh in FY22 and about 1.2 lakh in FY25, a fall of roughly 80 per cent in three years. Engineering campuses were built around that pipeline. When the pipeline narrowed, the campuses did not shrink with it.

We covered that shift in detail in [IT fresher hiring fell 80 per cent](/insights/blogs/it-fresher-hiring-india-2026), including where the hiring moved instead.

Undergraduate students also recorded the highest rate of offer disruption in the Unstop survey, at 17 per cent. An offer that is withdrawn or deferred is a particular kind of cruelty, because the student stops applying elsewhere the moment they receive it.

## What to do instead of waiting

**Run an off campus search in parallel, starting now.** Do not treat the placement cell as your process and off campus as a backup. Students who start applying outside only after campus placements fail have lost several months. [Off campus drives](/c/off-campus) run on their own calendar and do not care which college you attend.

**Go where the recruiters are not visiting.** Global Capability Centres grew their share of entry level hiring from 8 per cent to 11 per cent in a year, and they largely do not run mass campus drives. That is the whole reason their applicant pool is thinner. Our [GCC guide for freshers](/insights/playbook/how-to-get-into-a-gcc-as-a-fresher) covers how to apply.

**Use walk-in interviews.** A [walk-in](/c/walk-ins) skips the online screening stage entirely. You turn up, you are interviewed, and in many cases you know the outcome the same day. For a student with no campus offers and no shortlists, that is the shortest path from applying to an answer.

**Apply to seasonal hiring while you look.** India is adding up to 2.5 lakh seasonal roles before December, which we covered in [festive season jobs 2026](/insights/blogs/festive-season-jobs-2026-india). A three month role is not a career, but it is income, it is experience on a CV, and some of them convert.

**Widen the job titles you search for.** The report flags skill mismatch as a cause, but a large part of what looks like mismatch is vocabulary. Searching only for Software Engineer misses Operations Associate, Analyst, Process Associate and Trainee roles that you are already qualified for.

## One thing to be careful about

A difficult placement season is also when fake job offers increase. The rule is simple and has no exceptions. **No legitimate employer in India charges a candidate a registration fee, a security deposit or a training charge.** If money is being asked for, it is not a job.

We publish every listing we have [removed and why](/closed), and dead links and cancelled drives are the most common reasons.

## The short version

Companies are hiring and most students are not placed, because hiring is concentrated on campuses that recruiters already visit. If yours is not one of them, the campus process is not going to solve this for you, and the students getting placed this year are the ones running their own search alongside it.
