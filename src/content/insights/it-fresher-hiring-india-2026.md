If you are a fresher applying to the same six IT services companies as everyone in your batch, the pool you are competing for has shrunk by about four fifths in three years.

Here is the number that explains the last three placement seasons. According to staffing analytics firm Xpheno, Indian IT hired roughly **6 lakh freshers in FY22**. By **FY25 that figure was about 1.2 lakh**. That is a fall of close to **80 per cent**, and FY26 is expected to be only marginally better than FY25.

This is not a bad year that will correct itself. The shape of the industry changed.

## What actually caused it

Xpheno co-founder Kamal Karanth put it down to revenue, cost and margin pressure. Companies announce hiring numbers at the start of a year, and those numbers are partly a signal to investors rather than a commitment.

Harpreet Singh Saluja, president of NITES, made the same point more bluntly. Hiring numbers get announced to assure investors the business outlook is healthy, and not all of them materialise.

There is a second factor underneath that one. Entry level IT work has historically been testing, production support and basic programming. That is precisely the layer that automation reaches first. When a company says it is becoming AI first, the roles it stops opening are the ones a fresher used to start in.

## The revised numbers, company by company

The adjustments are visible in what the companies themselves have said:

| Company | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| Wipro | Fresher hiring guidance cut from about 10,000 to 7,500 to 8,000. Around 5,000 onboarded. |
| Infosys | Target of 20,000 freshers, with about 18,000 onboarded. |
| Tech Mahindra | Roughly 1,000 candidates waiting on joining letters. |
| Wipro | More than 200 recruits with onboarding deferred. |

Notice that the problem is not only fewer offers. It is also offers that take longer to turn into a joining date. If you are holding a letter and waiting, you are not alone, and it is worth reading our separate piece on [what to do when an offer letter has no joining date](/insights/blogs/offer-letter-no-joining-date-india).

## Where the hiring moved

This is the part that gets left out of most coverage, and it is the useful part.

Entry level hiring did not disappear. It moved to a different kind of employer. According to the foundit Insights Tracker for July 2026, Global Capability Centres raised their share of entry level hiring in India **from 8 per cent to 11 per cent** in a year. That was the largest gain of any employer category. Over the same period, traditional IT services firms fell **from 32 per cent to 24 per cent**.

A Global Capability Centre is an office a foreign company owns and staffs itself in India, rather than paying an Indian IT vendor to do that work. Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Goldman Sachs, SAP, Qualcomm and dozens of others run large centres here, mostly in Bengaluru and Hyderabad.

They do not visit most campuses. That is exactly why the applicant pool for them is smaller.

## What a fresher should actually do differently

**Stop treating the big IT services firms as the whole market.** They were about a third of entry level hiring and are now about a quarter. Apply to them, but do not only apply to them.

**Apply to operations and analyst titles, not only engineering ones.** GCC job titles rarely match campus vocabulary. Searching for Operations Associate or Analyst in Hyderabad returns more than searching for Software Engineer does.

**Widen beyond IT entirely.** Healthcare was the fastest growing function for freshers in the same tracker, rising from 7 per cent to 11 per cent of fresher hiring. Festive and seasonal hiring is adding up to 2.5 lakh roles before December, which we covered in [festive season jobs 2026](/insights/blogs/festive-season-jobs-2026-india).

**Check that a listing is still open before you spend time on it.** A shrinking market means more stale postings sitting on job sites long after the role closed. Every opening on [our live jobs page](/jobs) is re-checked daily, and we publish everything we have [taken down and why](/closed).

## The honest summary

Indian IT is hiring about a fifth of the freshers it hired in FY22. That is unlikely to reverse quickly, because the entry level work itself has changed rather than merely paused.

Entry level hiring has not gone away. It shifted to employers that do not run mass campus drives, which means it now rewards the candidate who looks beyond the companies that come to college.
